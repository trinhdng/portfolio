My story

I grew up around business in Ho Chi Minh City, one of Vietnam’s major economic hubs. My family has generations of business experience, so questions like how a business operates, how decisions are made and how opportunities are created were part of life long before I studied them. That curiosity shaped how I see the world, and it planted a wish to build something meaningful of my own.

In 2022, at 16, I moved to Australia to continue my education. It wasn’t easy, for me or for my family. I had to adapt to a new environment, become independent quickly and work through unfamiliar situations far from home. Over time Australia became my second home, and the experience made me more open-minded, adaptable and understanding of different perspectives.

My parents are a big reason I keep growing. They never placed heavy expectations on me or demanded a particular kind of success; they gave me the freedom to find my own path. That trust became my strongest motivation. I want to keep learning and become someone they can be proud of, not because I have to, but because I genuinely want to.

My family shaped my interest in business. Studying abroad shaped the person I am becoming.

Why business analytics

Business Analytics lets me combine business understanding, technology and problem-solving. What I enjoy most is taking a complex problem apart, understanding why it happens and finding where it can be improved. Data can reveal patterns that aren’t obvious at first, so decisions can rest on evidence instead of assumptions.

I’m also drawn to the people behind the numbers. Behind every dataset is a customer’s behaviour, a business challenge or a chance to create a better experience. I want to understand those different perspectives, talk with stakeholders and turn business needs into practical solutions.

To me, analytics is about asking the right questions, seeing the bigger picture and using evidence to make better decisions.

Peer mentoring

Volunteer, Business Peer Mentoring Program, Deakin University · October 2026 to present

I support new students as they settle in. As an international student at Deakin, I know how hard it can be to get used to a new environment, make friends and work through obstacles. In Deakin’s faculty peer mentoring model, new students are matched with an experienced student in the same or a similar course for their first five weeks of trimester. Mentors answer questions, share tips and connect mentees with campus services.

The program builds leadership, communication and teamwork skills, and it lets me use my own experience of moving abroad to help someone else feel at home.
